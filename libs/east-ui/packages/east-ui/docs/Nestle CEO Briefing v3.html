03 · The problem
The problem

An enterprise doesn't underperform because one decision is wrong.

It underperforms when many decisions across the value chain don't work together.

Forecasting, scheduling, production, rostering, procurement, inventory, recruitment, training. Each affects the impact of the others, but most are made in isolation and/or reactively.

The bottleneck moves, contention over resources grows, the opportunity costs compound. The problem re-appears somewhere else.

  • — Process success ≠ business wide success.
  • — Bottlenecks move, don't disappear.
  • — Each decision has its own data, its own truth.

07 · Use case A · Daily production decision support
Use case A · Daily production decision support

The decision at the facility, made with the full picture.

Every production unit, live. Every recommended action, explained. Every prediction, bounded by confidence. One place. One truth.

The decision

At any moment across the plant, production decision makers need to pick the next action — what to adjust, when, how much, at what setting — for each of hundreds of in-process units, each on its own multi-day process clock.

The problem

Throughput loss. Out-of-spec output. Rework and downgrade. Missed quality targets. Over- or under-correction. And the hardest one to measure: the better decision that never got made, because no one had time to think it through.

The solution

Every unit, live on one screen. Every recommended action weighs the full set of variables at play, looks ahead across the remaining process window, and anticipates problems before they become costly. Decision makers spend their time judging the call — not assembling the evidence.

08 · Use case B · Workforce optimisation
Use case B · Workforce optimisation

Coverage, cost, compliance — solved in one model.

Labour demand learned from the production plan. Coverage met at minimum total cost. Every EBA constraint respected by construction — not checked afterward.

The decision

Every week, the planner sets a roster across multiple operational areas. Each area has its own demand curve, its own qualified staff, its own shift patterns. The plan must cover production, respect every clause of the labour agreement, and cost as little as possible.

The problem

Over-coverage against a blurry demand signal. Expensive mix decisions made for peace of mind. Compliance handled by checklist, after the fact. Margin left on the table because "cheaper but still compliant" is too hard to find by hand. And the quiet default: build to safe, not to optimal.

The solution

Demand predicted from the production plan, not guessed. Coverage, cost and workforce mix optimised together. Every clause of the labour agreement encoded as a hard constraint — so the roster that comes out is the cheapest one that's also compliant. Planners spend their time on the judgement calls, not the arithmetic.
