Slots: Body is the children; stage is a component prop — both ultimately SubtypeExprOrValue<UIComponentType>; no keyframe type exists. <Story.Step>'s children render in the rail (mounted simultaneously, scrolling past), while exactly one step's stage renders in the sticky slot at a time — the same prop shape as Dialog's trigger. The split is the pattern — N scrolling prose blocks driving 1 persistent visual; collapse them and you have either an article (no stage) or text floating over a chart (no rail), both rejected above. body fills the step's full runway — the slot stretches to the block's height (stepLength), not just its content. Default flow is top-aligned, but the author's subtree owns the whole rectangle: vertically centre a pull-quote, pin a CTA to the block's foot, or space content across it with ordinary layout props (VStack justify). The runway is usable space, not dead margin. stage is optional — a step without one holds the previous keyframe, the natural shape for two beats narrating the same visual. eyebrow / title are prop sugar — typed shortcuts that guarantee the spec type treatment; a step with only children may compose its own. Rules: Type scale — eyebrow mono 9.5px / 600 / 0.18em caps (brand-d when active, ink-4 otherwise) · title DM Sans 17px / 600 / −0.01em ink · body 13px / 1.55 ink-2. One idea per step. A step body that needs a scrollbar is two steps. Soft cap: title + ~4 lines + one chip row. State is opacity only — inactive 0.45, active 1.0, 200ms ease-out. Text never translates or scales; motion budget belongs to the stage. Steps are clickable — clicking an inactive step smooth-scrolls it to the trigger line (the one sanctioned programmatic scroll). Interactive bodies are allowed — a final step routinely carries the call to action (<Button onClick={…}>Open the run →</Button>).
