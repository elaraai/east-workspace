§ 3

Use cases

Anchors · Decision.Loop · three decision tiers

The decision loop is one shape; the three tiers below bend it differently. Strategic decisions live for weeks and stage multiple scenarios in parallel — six stations (Explore merges Probe + Compare). Tactical decisions cycle weekly — the working surface (Roster, Planner) IS the canvas. Operational decisions are next-hour, audit-heavy, low ceremony. All three tiers run a 6-station loop — Probe and Compare merge into Explore (per-item Compare lives in the rail when applicable). Each tier’s rules-block names how its variant of the loop differs from the base; the worked example shows the surfaces, charts, and Slice/Scenario chrome that actually fit. Slice and Scenario chrome (Range, Cohort, Scenario.Switch) is visible in every mini-frame header per §1.5c-e.

The decision loop · seven stations

How the Decide family composes with Configure, Slice, Scenario, and Trust during a single operator decision. Seven steps, one loop · Decide owns the canvas · Configure provides primitives, never owns the canvas during a decision. Same architectural shape as the other §1.5 sections: one handle per concern, named back-edges, no silent context switches.

Per-step matrixWhich pattern owns each step · what it asks · what's embeddable.
Step Question Anchor pattern Forward → Jumps ↺
01 Triage What needs me? Decision.Queue → 02 —
02 Understand What's the model proposing, and why? Scenario.Briefing → 03 — (receives ↺ from Probe)
03 Probe What if I changed assumption X? Scenario.Probe (new) → 04 ↺ 02 on tweak-settled
04 Compare A vs B vs current state? Diff.View → 05 ↺ 06 skip-judge if attested
05 Judge Do I stand behind the load-bearing inputs? Judgement prompt inside Briefing → 06 — (gates 06, never skipped)
06 Commit Apply · Modify · Override · Defer. Commit.Bar → 07 ↺ 03 on Modify
07 Communicate Tell affected people. Communicate.Handoff · Communicate.Message ↻ 01 loop closes —
Reading the matrix: Forward → is the canonical happy path arrow drawn around the outside of the loop diagram. Jumps ↺ are the back-edges drawn as dashed inner arrows — three of them: Probe → Understand when a tweak settles and the briefing re-renders, Compare → Commit when the operator skips re-judgement because the load-bearing inputs were already attested, Modify → Probe when Commit.Bar's Modify expands the briefing in place. Judge has no jump out — it gates Commit and is never skipped. Communicate's forward arrow closes the loop (↻) back to Triage for the next case.
Composition rulesHow Decide relates to Configure, Slice, Scenario, and Trust.
  • Decide owns the canvas during a decision. The decision context — case-id, current scenario, judgement state, alternatives — lives on the Scenario.Briefing surface and never leaves it. The operator can probe, compare, even open a sub-popover, but the briefing chassis stays put.
  • Configure provides primitives, never owns the canvas during a decision. Lever.Slider, Input.Assumptions, Cause.SupplyDemand, planner cells — all designed to be embeddable in compact density inside Scenario.Probe. Configure as a destination mode still exists, but only for global policies and defaults that aren't tied to a single decision.
  • Modify in Commit.Bar opens Probe in place. Never navigates to Configure mode. Re-flowing the briefing to expose 2-3 load-bearing controls is the right shape — the operator's muscle memory for the case stays intact, and the re-solve happens against the same DecisionHandle.
  • Back-edges are first-class, not undo paths. Returning from Probe to Understand is a deliberate operation that snaps the briefing to its post-tweak state, not a navigation-back. Compare → Commit skips re-judgement only when the operator has already attested to the load-bearing inputs in step 5. The renderer enforces these transitions through the DecisionHandle's state.
  • One DecisionHandle per case. The handle carries the case-id, the active scenario (which is itself a ScenarioHandle), the judgement-prompt answer, and the commit state (clean / dirty / applied / overridden). Every step reads it; only certain steps write specific fields. Like SliceHandle and ScenarioHandle, it binds once at the surface boundary.
  • Slice and Scenario compose underneath. A Decision.Queue uses a SliceHandle to narrow which cases the operator sees. A Scenario.Briefing uses a ScenarioHandle to choose which proposed future is in play. Decide doesn't replace these — it sits on top of them. Editing a slice doesn't dirty the decision; switching the scenario does.
  • Trust journal entries record back-edges. Every Probe edit, every Compare swap, every Override is journalled with the case-id. The audit trail captures not just the final commit but the path through the loop — what was probed, what was rejected, why the operator chose what they did. This is what makes the back-edges first-class rather than disposable.
InvariantsHard rules every Decide surface must honour.
  • No mode-switch on Modify. Modify opens Scenario.Probe inside the briefing. Configure-as-destination is reserved for global-policy work that has no case-id.
  • Probe is bounded by sensitivity. The Probe panel only exposes inputs flagged as load-bearing by Stat.Sensitivity. Operators don't get a free-form Configure mini-mode; they get the 2-5 controls that actually move the recommendation. Anything else requires a deliberate mode switch.
  • Judgement gates Commit. Apply is disabled until the judgement prompt is answered. On No, commit is blocked entirely; on Don't know, the case routes to Communicate.Handoff. On Yes, Apply enables and an attestation chip materialises on the audit trail.
  • The loop closes. After Commit (or Override, or Defer, or Handoff), the surface returns the operator to Triage — never dead-ends on "applied." Failing to close the loop is the most common UX mistake in decision tools; the renderer enforces it.
  • DecisionHandle is URL-bindable. A deep-link restores the case-id, the active scenario, any in-flight probe edits, and the position in the loop (e.g. "opened, briefing read, probe-day-8-DAP edited, awaiting judgement"). Operators can hand off mid-decision without losing context.
Strategic

Strategic decisions · weeks-to-quarters

How the loop applies
  • Time horizon. weeks to quarters. Decisions stay alive for days; multiple scenarios coexist in a portfolio.
  • Six stations, not seven. Probe and Compare merge into Explore (Probe + Compare). Strategic users do both repeatedly; the line between them is fuzzy at this horizon.
  • Chart-heavy Understand. Charts (Chart.Line projection · Chart.Bar tornado · Chart.Sankey blend · Chart.Heatmap matrix) carry the narrative. The Briefing is a chart caption, not the surface.
  • Judgement is a persistent sidebar checklist. Multiple constraints stay visible throughout Explore and Commit · attested individually · never a single prompt.
  • Commit stages a rollout. Apply is not "do it now" — it stages a phased plan (Collections.Gantt of sequenced changes) routed across multiple quarters.
  • Slice + Scenario constant. Slice.Range (the FY horizon) + Scenario.Switch (active scenario across A · B · C · D) visible on every step. See §1.5c-e.

Tactical

Tactical decisions · days-to-week

How the loop applies
  • Time horizon. days to one week. Decisions cycle weekly.
  • Canvas IS the decision. The Roster surface is where the work happens. Briefing reduces to supporting evidence in the rail.
  • Probe is direct manipulation. Drag shifts, drop subs from the Library, watch KPIs and demand-vs-supply charts recompute. No separate Probe overlay.
  • Charts complement, don't dominate. Chart.Area (Cause.SupplyDemand · demand fcst) · Chart.Bar (per-team wage) sit beside the Roster, not on top.
  • Judgement is inline. Prompts appear on the cells they relate to ("Patel 6 consec days"), attested in place.
  • Commit routes communication. Apply queues Communicate.Swap proposals to affected staff. The decision isn't final until counter-parties respond.
  • Six stations, not seven. Probe and Compare merge into Explore (Probe + Compare). Direct manipulation IS probing; per-item Compare (cell-level disagreement A vs B) appears in the rail when the operator selects a cell.

Operational

Operational decisions · next-hour-to-shift

How the loop applies
  • Time horizon. next hour or next shift. The decision is now.
  • Low ceremony. Probe and Compare are rare. Operator usually accepts or rejects the model's suggestion outright.
  • Audit-heavy. Every action logs a Trust.Journal entry immediately on commit. The audit trail is the operational record.
  • Chart.Line dominates Understand. Trajectory (Chart.Line · Stat.BeforeAfter) over time is the operator's primary read · Schematic shows the current state · everything else is supporting.
  • Judgement is a safety check. One prompt: dosage in band, no cross-contamination, no conflict. Yes or No.
  • Communicate is annotation. Brief shift-handover note attached to the journal entry · Communicate.Annotate, not a Handoff packet.
