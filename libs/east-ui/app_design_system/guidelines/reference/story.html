§ 2.7

Narrate

Anchors · Story.Root · Story.Stage

Observe answers "what's happening". Narrate answers "let me walk you through it" — an analysis handed to a stakeholder, a postmortem, a model-run explanation, an onboarding tour. One persistent visual (the stage) evolves through keyframes while prose steps scroll past in a rail beside it. The reader's scrollbar is the timeline.

StanceWhat separates this from marketing-page scrollytelling.
Non-negotiables: Text never floats over the visual. Prose lives in a dedicated rail beside the stage — left-aligned, on paper, at body sizes. No centered captions, no translucent cards over the chart, no text-shadow. The Flourish/news-site overlay style is explicitly out: it forces short captions, hides data under the caption, and breaks at every breakpoint. Native scroll only. The component observes scroll position (IntersectionObserver semantics); it never hijacks the wheel, never snaps, never animates scrollTop on its own. A reader can fling past the whole story and nothing fights them. The single exception: clicking a step / progress dot may smooth-scroll to that step, because the reader asked. The stage owns no chrome. It is a fixed-height sticky slot; a keyframe is any SubtypeExprOrValue<UIComponentType> rendered into it, directly on the page background. A keyframe that wants the Card treatment composes <Card> like anywhere else — there is no frame prop to learn. The stage's height is fixed per story so keyframe tweens never reflow the page. Steps and stage are ordinary components. A step body is any east-ui subtree (Text, MetricChip, Stat.Card, even a Button). The stage is any east-ui subtree per keyframe — usually a Chart, but a Table, Matrix or Planner keyframe is equally valid. Story adds narrative state and motion; it owns no visuals of its own. Every keyframe renders deterministically. activeStep forces a static state — the snapshot pipeline renders one PNG per step, and deep links (#story=q4-review/promo) land on a specific keyframe.

Slots: Body is the children; stage is a component prop — both ultimately SubtypeExprOrValue<UIComponentType>; no keyframe type exists. <Story.Step>'s children render in the rail (mounted simultaneously, scrolling past), while exactly one step's stage renders in the sticky slot at a time — the same prop shape as Dialog's trigger. The split is the pattern — N scrolling prose blocks driving 1 persistent visual; collapse them and you have either an article (no stage) or text floating over a chart (no rail), both rejected above. body fills the step's full runway — the slot stretches to the block's height (stepLength), not just its content. Default flow is top-aligned, but the author's subtree owns the whole rectangle: vertically centre a pull-quote, pin a CTA to the block's foot, or space content across it with ordinary layout props (VStack justify). The runway is usable space, not dead margin. stage is optional — a step without one holds the previous keyframe, the natural shape for two beats narrating the same visual. eyebrow / title are prop sugar — typed shortcuts that guarantee the spec type treatment; a step with only children may compose its own. Rules: Type scale — eyebrow mono 9.5px / 600 / 0.18em caps (brand-d when active, ink-4 otherwise) · title DM Sans 17px / 600 / −0.01em ink · body 13px / 1.55 ink-2. One idea per step. A step body that needs a scrollbar is two steps. Soft cap: title + ~4 lines + one chip row. State is opacity only — inactive 0.45, active 1.0, 200ms ease-out. Text never translates or scales; motion budget belongs to the stage. Steps are clickable — clicking an inactive step smooth-scrolls it to the trigger line (the one sanctioned programmatic scroll). Interactive bodies are allowed — a final step routinely carries the call to action (<Button onClick={…}>Open the run →</Button>).
Story.Stage Anchor

The sticky slot that holds one keyframe per step. A keyframe is an ordinary east-ui subtree; the renderer owns the motion between adjacent keyframes. Authors write four plain charts — the renderer makes them one evolving chart.

Tween contract (renderer-owned): Same mark family, same x-type → continuous tween: scales/domains animate 360ms ease-out; series are matched by name — matched series tween geometry, entering series fade+draw in, exiting series fade out first. Annotations (refLine / refBand / refDot) enter by sliding from their axis, 240ms, after series settle. Different component kinds (Line → Bar, Chart → Table) → crossfade 200ms, old out then new in, frame size constant. Scrubbed properties — a keyframe may read the progress binding (Float 0–1 within the step) for scroll-locked effects: progressive data reveal (Chart.window(p)), a sweeping refLine. Scrub maps 1:1 to scroll — no easing, no duration; easing a scrub makes the chart lag the reader's hand. Reduced motion — prefers-reduced-motion ⇒ every transition becomes a 120ms crossfade and scrubbed reveals render complete.
Discipline: Keep one visual anchor across keyframes. Adjacent keyframes should share an axis, a series, or a frame region — a story where every beat swaps the whole visual is a Carousel wearing a trenchcoat. Framing belongs to keyframes, not the stage. Keyframes own their full rectangle; one that wants a border composes <Card>. Keep adjacent keyframes' framing consistent — all bare or all carded — so the stage reads as one object while it tweens, and give bare stages ≥ 32px clearance from surrounding page chrome. The stage never carries chrome. No Slice affordances, no toolbars inside the stage — the narrative is the filter. If the reader needs to explore, the story's last step links out to the Observe surface with the slice pre-bound.
Story.Progress

The one-row chrome strip: eyebrow title · per-step dots · counter · prev/next. Optional inside Story.Root; also mounts standalone anywhere (a page header, a Drawer foot) because it only talks to the active binding.

Rules: One row, never wraps (§Overflow). Past ~12 steps the dots compress to a 64px segmented meter; the counter stays. Dots are buttons — click and prev/next simply write the active binding; the rail treats the write as navigation and smooth-scrolls. Keyboard — when the story has focus: ↑/↓ or j/k step; Home/End jump. Focus never traps — Tab exits normally. States — dot 6px: rule-strong upcoming · brand @ 0.5 seen · brand-d current.
WiringStory is state. The rail is a writer; everything else may read.

Same idiom as Stat.Layout: the narrative position is one ordinary state binding — an IntegerType index, usually from State.bind, but anything exposing read/write closures works. There is no bespoke handle and nothing to bind when you don't need it: omit the prop and Story keeps the state internal. Pass it only when something outside the story must read or drive the position — then it behaves like any other state: readable in any <Reactive>, writable from a Button three components away, snapshot-able, deep-linkable.

Wiring · in code
// One legal program: $.const / $.let exist only inside an East.function
// builder (orderRows / uplift: raw row data).
const QuarterReview = East.function([], UIComponentType, ($) => {

  // 0 · Rows are typed once. Every field reference below — chart accessors,
  //     Table column keys — is checked against this struct, exactly as in
  //     Table / Chart today. Story adds no stringly-typed data surface.
  const Order  = StructType({ week: IntegerType, actual: FloatType, forecast: FloatType });
  const orders = $.const(orderRows, ArrayType(Order));

  // 1 · OPTIONAL — plain State bindings. Omit the props and Story manages its
  //     position internally; bind only when other surfaces must read or drive
  //     it (same idiom as Stat.Layout's selection).
  const active = $.let(State.bind([IntegerType], "q4-review.step", 0n));
  const scrub  = $.let(State.bind([FloatType],   "q4-review.progress", 0.0));

  // 2 · Shared layers are plain build-time values — declare once, reference
  //     from several keyframes. The renderer tweens series matched by name.
  const actual   = Chart.Line(orders, { x: d => d.week, y: d => d.actual },   { name: "actual",   color: "teal.solid" });
  const forecast = Chart.Line(orders, { x: d => d.week, y: d => d.forecast }, { name: "forecast", color: "gray.solid", dash: "4 3" });

  // 3 · Steps are children. <Story.Step> is a real component — its children
  //     are the rail body, its stage prop is the keyframe (a UIComponent
  //     prop, same shape as Dialog's trigger). The renderer routes bodies →
  //     rail and the active stage → the sticky slot, like Tabs shows one panel.
  // 4 · Anything can read or drive the position — it's just State. A write
  //     from outside is navigation: the rail smooth-scrolls to that step.
  return (
    <VStack gap="6">
      <Story active={active} progress={scrub} layout="rail-left" stageHeight={420} stepLength="default"
             onStepEnter={East.function([StringType], NullType, ($, id) => { … })}>
        <Story.Step id="demand" eyebrow="Demand" title="Demand ran hot"
                    stage={<Chart layers={[actual]} grid />}>
          <Text>Orders climbed from week 6 and never gave the gain back…</Text>
          <MetricChip sentiment="pos">▲ +34% vs start of Q4</MetricChip>
        </Story.Step>
        <Story.Step id="forecast" eyebrow="Forecast" title="The plan missed the turn"
                    stage={<Chart layers={[actual, forecast]} grid legend />}>
          …
        </Story.Step>
        <Story.Step id="promo" eyebrow="Cause" title="One event explains the spike"
                    stage={<Chart layers={[actual, Chart.refDot({ x: 8n, y: 1010, label: "promo live" })]} grid />}>
          …
        </Story.Step>
        <Story.Step id="regions" eyebrow="Where" title="The gap wasn't uniform"
                    stage={<Chart layers={Chart.Bar(uplift, { x: d => d.region, y: d => d.orders, by: d => d.half })} grid legend />}>
          …
        </Story.Step>
      </Story>
      <Reactive>{$ => {
        const i = $.let(active.read());
        const next = $.const(East.function([], NullType, $ => { $(active.write(active.read().add(1n))); }));
        return <HStack gap="3">
          <Text>{East.str`Reviewing beat ${i.add(1n)} of 4`}</Text>
          <Button onClick={next}>Skip ahead →</Button>
        </HStack>;
      }}</Reactive>
      <Story.Progress active={active} count={4} />
    </VStack>
  );
});
Binding contract: active — IntegerType read/write closures. The scroll driver writes it as steps cross the trigger line; an external write is treated as navigation — the rail smooth-scrolls that step to the trigger line, then resumes observing. Reads track dependencies inside <Reactive> like any other state. progress — optional FloatType binding, 0–1 through the active step. Written by the scroll driver only; consumers read it for scrub effects and never write it. Defaults — omit both and Story manages internal state; the component is fully usable with zero wiring. No Story.bind, no handle type — there is nothing to learn beyond State.bind.
DX rules: Steps are children. <Story> is a children-taking container like ChipRail / Stack, and <Story.Step> is a real component — a UIComponentType variant arm carrying id / eyebrow / title, its children as the rail body, and its keyframe on the stage prop (a UIComponent prop, the established Dialog.trigger shape). The contract "children must be Story.Steps" is soft, exactly like ChipRail's chip-shaped children — the renderer skips non-step children with a dev warning. Routing and branching live in the renderer. Children are runtime values, so Story's renderer — not the IR builder — walks them: bodies render in document order in the rail, the active step's stage renders in the sticky slot, and the switch on the active index happens at render time, the way Tabs' renderer shows one panel. Authors never write the switch; and because steps are values, a conditional step (cond.ifElse(<Story.Step…/>, <Story.Step…/>)) is legal. Inference is inherited, not reinvented. Keyframes are plain <Chart> / <Table> values, so the existing typed surfaces apply unchanged: chart encodings are typed accessors (d => d.week) checked against the row struct, Table columns are keyed by the row's fields (ColumnSpec generic), and shared layers are ordinary build-time consts reused across keyframes. The only strings Story itself introduces are the per-step ids, used for deep links and snapshot names; because steps are runtime children, activeStep is a plain StringType (no static key union — the accepted price of the children form). East-function handlers — onStepEnter / onStepExit(id) fire on transitions in either direction; use for analytics, prefetching a heavy keyframe's data, or pinging a sibling surface. Scrub is just Reactive. A keyframe that wants scroll-locked effects wraps its subtree in <Reactive> and reads the progress binding — no new mechanism. Standalone chrome — <Story.Progress active={active} /> mounts anywhere (a page header, a Drawer foot) because it shares nothing with the Story but the binding.
Static & pipeline: activeStep="promo" — overrides scroll, renders one deterministic keyframe; the snapshot pipeline emits one PNG per step (story/q4-review/demand.png, …). Deep link — #story=q4-review/promo writes the active binding on mount and scrolls the step to the trigger line. Print — linearizes: each step renders full-ink with its keyframe inline below it; the spine becomes the left margin rule.
Story vs …Pick the cheapest pattern that fits.
Decision rule: vs Tabs — Tabs are random-access and stateless between visits; Story is ordered and persistent. If beats can be read in any order, use Tabs. vs Carousel — Carousel pages siblings of equal weight with no shared visual; Story evolves one visual. If nothing persists across beats, it's a Carousel. vs Stat.Annotated — one chart + event markers needs no narrative chrome. Reach for Story only when the sequence of readings carries the argument. vs an Observe page — Story is for the rendered conclusion of an analysis, not for exploration. If the reader should be able to re-slice, give them Observe with a Slice handle and link to it from the story's last step.
